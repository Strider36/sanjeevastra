# Primary Healthcare Hypertension Screening and Referral Protocol

## 1. Diagnostic Thresholds and Staging
Hypertension is defined as a persistent resting blood pressure where systolic blood pressure (SBP) is 140 mmHg or greater, and/or diastolic blood pressure (DBP) is 90 mmHg or greater, measured on at least two separate occasions.
- **Normal**: SBP < 120 mmHg and DBP < 80 mmHg.
- **Pre-hypertension / Elevated**: SBP 120-129 mmHg and DBP < 80 mmHg.
- **Stage 1 Hypertension**: SBP 130-139 mmHg or DBP 80-89 mmHg.
- **Stage 2 Hypertension**: SBP 140 mmHg or higher, or DBP 90 mmHg or higher.
- **Hypertensive Emergency**: SBP > 180 mmHg and/or DBP > 120 mmHg with target organ damage.

## 2. Emergency Hypertensive Crisis
Immediate emergency referral (Medical SOS / 112) is mandatory if severe BP elevation (SBP > 180 or DBP > 120 mmHg) occurs alongside acute symptoms:
- Crushing central chest pain radiating to the left arm or jaw.
- Sudden shortness of breath (acute pulmonary edema).
- Sudden severe headache, visual disturbances, or neurological signs indicative of stroke.
- Confusion or acute encephalopathy.

## 3. Lifestyle Interventions and Primary Care Management
For Stage 1 hypertension without end-organ compromise:
- **Dietary Sodium Reduction**: Limit salt intake to less than 5 grams per day (approximately one level teaspoon).
- **DASH Diet Principles**: Increase dietary intake of fresh vegetables, fruits, whole grains, nuts, and low-fat dairy.
- **Aerobic Physical Activity**: Minimum of 150 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise weekly (e.g., brisk walking, cycling).
- **Weight Management**: Maintain a target body mass index (BMI) within normal demographic ranges.
- **Tobacco and Alcohol Avoidance**: Complete cessation of smoking and tobacco products.
