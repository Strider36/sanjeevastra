# Clinical Triage and Care Navigation Guidelines for Acute Headaches

## 1. Introduction and Clinical Objective
Headaches represent one of the most frequent reasons for outpatient medical consultation and emergency triage. The primary clinical objective in healthcare navigation is differentiating benign primary headache syndromes (such as tension-type headaches and episodic migraine) from potentially life-threatening secondary headaches requiring immediate trauma or neurological intervention.

## 2. Red Flag Symptoms (Emergency Medical Dispatch)
Patients presenting with any of the following "SNOOP" red flag criteria must be directed immediately to an emergency department or have Medical SOS dispatched (National Emergency 112 / Ambulance 108):
- **Systemic signs**: Fever, unexplained weight loss, night sweats, or known malignancy.
- **Neurological deficits**: Focal weakness, sensory loss, dysphasia, visual field defects, or altered level of consciousness.
- **Onset pattern**: Sudden, explosive onset reaching maximum intensity within seconds to one minute ("thunderclap headache").
- **Older age**: New headache onset after age 50 years.
- **Pattern change**: Significant progression in frequency, severity, or associated neck stiffness and confusion.

## 3. Moderate Symptoms (Primary Health Centre Referral)
Patients without red flags who experience any of the following should be scheduled for clinical evaluation at the nearest Primary Health Centre (PHC) or clinic within 24 to 48 hours:
- Headaches lasting more than 72 consecutive hours without relief.
- Frequent unilateral throbbing headaches accompanied by moderate nausea or photophobia (consistent with migraine).
- Headaches triggered consistently by coughing, bending, or physical exertion.
- Inadequate symptom control despite standard over-the-counter analgesic therapy.

## 4. Mild Symptoms and Supportive Home Care
Patients experiencing mild, bilateral, band-like tension headaches without neurological symptoms may monitor their condition with supportive care:
- Adequate oral hydration (2 to 2.5 liters of clean water daily).
- Rest in a quiet, dark environment with stress-reduction techniques.
- Cold or warm compress application to the forehead or neck.
- Short-term over-the-counter analgesia (e.g., paracetamol) taken strictly according to manufacturer dosage guidelines.
- If symptoms persist beyond 3 days, clinical evaluation is advised.
